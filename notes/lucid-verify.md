# Lucid Verify — Product Concept

**Status:** Concept — June 2026 (updated June 30, 2026)
**Origin:** Conversation with Shawn MacDonald, CEO, Verité (June 25, 2026)

---

## The Problem

Recruitment fee debt bondage is the most pervasive form of forced labor in global supply chains. Workers — primarily low-wage migrants in Southeast Asia, South Asia, and the Gulf — pay exorbitant fees to recruitment agents to secure jobs abroad, often going into debt before they earn a single paycheck. International standards, including the ILO's Employer Pays Principle, require employers to bear the full cost of ethical recruitment. Most don't.

The compliance gap is partly an enforcement problem and partly an evidence problem. Brands and buyers conducting forced labor due diligence require suppliers to prove they paid recruitment fees on behalf of workers. In practice, this means suppliers upload PDFs — recruitment contracts and bank transfer records — to compliance platforms. PDFs can be fabricated. Verité's own CUMULUS data shows that the "overwhelming majority" of suppliers who claim to pay fees demonstrably did not pay all quantifiable costs, despite submitting documentary evidence.

**The verification system is only as trustworthy as the documents it accepts. Right now, it isn't.**

---

## The Solution

Lucid Verify replaces the document upload workflow with a payment-first verification model. Instead of paying a recruiter through normal banking channels and uploading a PDF afterward, the employer initiates the recruiter fee payment directly through Lucid Verify's interface. The payment is processed by a licensed, regulated third-party financial institution. The transaction confirmation — generated by that institution, not by the employer — becomes the verified record submitted to the compliance platform.

The evidence is no longer a self-reported document. It is an institutional record generated by a licensed payment provider with no incentive to falsify it.

---

## How It Works

**1. Employer creates a placement request**
The employer logs into Lucid Verify, creates a placement (specifying role, cohort size, country, fee terms) and either handles hiring directly or assigns it to a licensed recruiter account.

**2. Recruiter posts the job and manages candidates**
The recruiter receives the job order, activates it as a job listing visible to workers, and manages the candidate pipeline — applications, screening, selection. Workers reach the listing via two paths: direct-apply (worker finds the job on the platform and applies) or agency-initiated (sub-agents refer workers to the listing). Both paths converge at the same contract and payment flow.

**3. Contract generated and signed on-platform**
Once candidates are selected, Lucid Verify generates a digital employment contract between employer, recruiter, and worker. The contract explicitly states the agreed recruiter fee (paid by the employer) and that the worker owes zero fees. Workers sign via mobile. The contract is timestamped and stored — ESIGN-compliant, no blockchain required in V1.

**4. Employer pays recruiter fee through Lucid Verify**
The employer initiates the recruiter fee payment through Lucid Verify's interface. The payment is processed by Wise Platform, a licensed payment provider operating in 80+ countries. Wise handles money movement through local banking rails. The employer never interacts with Wise directly.

**5. Lucid Verify captures the transaction record**
On payment completion, Lucid Verify receives a transaction confirmation from Wise's API: transaction ID, amount, currency, timestamp, sender, recipient. This record is stored and linked to the contract and placement.

**6. Verified record submitted to the compliance platform**
Lucid Verify submits both the contract record and the transaction confirmation to the buyer's compliance platform (e.g., CUMULUS) in lieu of PDF uploads. The compliance platform can independently verify the payment by querying Wise's API using the transaction ID. No trust in Lucid required; the record is independently auditable.

**7. Worker record accessible on-platform**
Workers retain mobile access to their contract throughout the engagement. The zero-fee terms are visible at any time, giving workers leverage if a recruiter or sub-agent attempts to charge them after placement.

## Why This Is the Right Wedge

The recruiter fee payment use case avoids the barriers that make other Lucid products harder to deploy in the near term:

- **No informality tension.** Unlike regular wages — where workers sometimes prefer cash for flexibility — recruiter fee payments are formal, one-time transactions. Both employer and worker want them documented.
- **No competing incumbent system.** There is no established "how employers pay recruiters" platform to displace. Lucid Verify can be the native rail.
- **Both parties have incentive to adopt.** The employer proves compliance; the worker (or their advocate) proves they weren't charged.
- **Regulatory clarity.** Fiat payments through Wise avoid the crypto regulatory barriers that affect Lucid Ledger's core product in South Asia and other target markets.
- **No blockchain required.** The verification integrity comes from Wise's institutional record, not from on-chain anchoring. This is buildable now, without depending on infrastructure that doesn't yet exist.

## Platform Architecture

Lucid Verify introduces a three-party model — employer, recruiter, and worker — with hiring and payment functions explicitly separated.

**Role separation:**
The "hiring party" (who posts jobs, manages candidates, generates contracts) and the "paying party" (who initiates payments) are distinct roles that can be held by the same actor or different ones. An employer who self-recruits holds both. An employer who uses a licensed recruiter delegates the hiring function; the payment function remains with the employer. This modularity is the core architectural contribution.

**Login types:** Employer / Recruiter / Worker / Mediator / Admin

**Employer dashboard:** Active placements (recruiter assigned, worker count, payment status, CUMULUS verification status); payments due to recruiters; verified payment records for CUMULUS download; create new placement → assign to recruiter or self-hire.

**Recruiter dashboard:** Job orders received from employers; active listings; candidate pipeline (applied → screened → selected → contracted); contracts pending worker signature; completed placements; fees received.

**Worker dashboard:** Browse jobs (direct-apply path); my applications; my contracts (view, sign, zero-fee terms visible); fee confirmation record.

**Two worker entry paths (not mutually exclusive):**
- *Direct-apply:* Worker finds a job on the platform and applies independently, without going through a sub-agent.
- *Agency-initiated:* Sub-agent or recruiter brings workers to the platform to apply for a specific listing. The worker's first touch with Lucid may be receiving a contract link to sign.

Sub-agent referral fees can be programmed as bonus payments in a future iteration; this is scoped out of V1.

## Technical Architecture

**Codebase:** Lucid Verify is a fork of Lucid Ledger (see Relationship to Lucid Ledger below). The blockchain payment layer (USDC escrow on Base) is replaced by the Wise fiat payment layer. All other platform infrastructure — authentication (Privy), PostgreSQL backend, role-based access, contract generation, admin dashboard — is inherited from Lucid Ledger with modifications for the recruiter role.

**Payment layer:** Wise Platform API. Lucid applies to Wise's fintech partnership program. Wise handles licensing, FX, local banking rails, and compliance in each operating country.

**Record layer:** PostgreSQL database storing contract records, transaction IDs, and metadata (employer, recruiter, worker, amount, date, associated compliance assessment, placement ID), linked together at the placement level.

**Integration layer:** API connection to CUMULUS (and future platforms) submitting contract and transaction records in place of PDF uploads. Compliance platforms can independently verify payment records via Wise's API using the transaction ID.

**Authentication:** Privy (inherited from Lucid Ledger) handles employer, recruiter, and worker login. The embedded wallet functionality in Privy is unused in Lucid Verify; only the auth and identity layer is active.

**No blockchain in V1.** Verification integrity derives from Wise's institutional record and the on-platform contract. Blockchain anchoring can be added in a future version if a use case demands independently verifiable records without depending on Wise's infrastructure.

## Regulatory Considerations

**Lucid Verify's regulatory exposure is minimal if structured correctly.**

The critical design principle: Lucid must never hold or transmit funds. Money flows from the employer's account through Wise to the recruiter. Lucid sends instructions to Wise's API; it is not a counterparty to the payment. In this structure, Lucid is a software company, not a money transmitter.

- **FinCEN (US):** Likely no MSB registration required if Lucid holds no funds. Verify with a payments lawyer before launch.
- **Operating countries:** Wise's existing licenses cover the operating jurisdictions (Philippines, Indonesia, Bangladesh, Nepal, Malaysia, Gulf states). Lucid's exposure as a US software company to local payment licensing is minimal in this structure.
- **EU/UK:** PSD2 payment initiation service licensing could apply if Lucid expands to European employers. Not an immediate concern.

**Key action:** Negotiate Wise Platform partnership terms such that Lucid acts as Wise's agent (under Wise's license umbrella) rather than as a principal. This is a standard structure in Wise's partner program.

**Legal review:** Budget $5,000–$10,000 for a payments regulatory lawyer to review the Wise partnership agreement and confirm the regulatory structure before launch.

## Business Model

**Revenue:** Per-verification fee charged to CUMULUS members (or to employers using the standalone product) each time a recruiter fee payment is processed through Lucid Verify. Alternatively, an annual API access fee for compliance platforms integrating Lucid Verify.

**Pricing rationale:** Recruitment fees in target markets typically range from $1,000 to $5,000+ per worker. A per-verification fee of $10–$50 is negligible relative to the compliance value and the cost of a Verité CUMULUS membership.

**Distribution:** The Verité/CUMULUS partnership provides immediate access to 350+ employers and 750+ recruiters without direct sales effort. Subsequent expansion to other compliance platforms (EcoVadis, ELEVATE, Sedex) replicates this model.

## Relationship to Lucid Ledger

Lucid Verify and Lucid Ledger share the same codebase and follow an open-core architecture under Lucid Strategies PBC.

**Lucid Ledger** is the upstream open-source platform (AGPL-3.0) where all core development happens. Every new platform feature — including the recruiter role, three-party placement model, and modular payment layer — is built into Lucid Ledger first. Lucid Ledger uses blockchain-based payment infrastructure (USDC escrow on Base, oracle verification, dispute resolution) and targets a controlled academic pilot in the Philippines.

**Lucid Verify** is a downstream commercial fork of Lucid Ledger. The blockchain payment layer is replaced by Wise fiat payments; the CUMULUS integration is added. All other platform infrastructure is inherited. Lucid Verify is issued under a commercial license by Lucid Strategies PBC, not AGPL — this is permitted because Lucid Strategies is the copyright holder of the Lucid Ledger codebase and can dual-license it.

**Dual-licensing model:** Lucid Ledger is available under AGPL for anyone who wants to use or contribute openly. Commercial licenses (issued by Lucid Strategies PBC) are required for anyone building proprietary products on the codebase — including Lucid Verify itself. This mirrors the model used by MongoDB, GitLab, and Elastic: open-source community version drives adoption and contributions; commercial license generates revenue and prevents free-riding by competitors.

**Development flow:** Build in Lucid Ledger → fork and swap payment layer → Lucid Verify. Future white-label products follow the same pattern with different payment layers or vertical integrations.

**Why this structure:**
- Recruiter role and platform architecture development serves both products simultaneously — no duplicated effort
- AGPL moat means competitors cannot fork Lucid Ledger to build a competing Lucid Verify without open-sourcing their version or paying for a commercial license
- Lucid Verify's commercial success funds continued open-source development on Lucid Ledger
- The two products are complementary: Lucid Verify builds brand and supply chain relationships near-term while Lucid Ledger's blockchain infrastructure matures

- Migrations 028–030, correctly registered in `server.js`'s startup list

**Not yet built:** candidate pipeline (applied → screened → selected → contracted — currently just a flat assigned-jobs list), three-party contract generation, and any UI for recording a recruiter fee payment (`createRecruiterFeePayment`/`getRecruiterFeePayments` exist as bare API client methods but nothing calls them).

**Immediate next step:** rebuild the Docker containers and manually test the existing scaffold (signup → assignment → dashboard) end-to-end before building anything further on top of it.

### Reuse Assessment for the Fork

**Directly reusable as-is:** Privy auth stack (`useAuth`, JWKS verification middleware), Express/Sequelize/PostgreSQL architecture and migration-runner pattern, `Employee`/`Employer`/`JobPosting`/`JobApplication`/`SavedJob` models, `Recruiter`/`RecruiterFeePayment` models, frontend routing/layout/`ProtectedRoute` conventions, admin dashboard pattern.

**Must be stripped out:** `client/src/contracts/` (aaClient, deployViaFactory, workContractInteractions, all ABIs), `DeployedContract`/`OracleVerification`/`PaymentTransaction`/`KioskDevice`/`NfcBadge`/`PresenceEvent`/`QrToken` models and their controllers/routes/pages, `Mediator`/`DisputeHistory` (escrow-dispute specific — a one-time recruiter fee payment doesn't need DAO-style arbitration), `ethers`/`viem`/`permissionless` dependencies.

**Net-new work needed (doesn't exist in either repo yet):** Wise API integration, three-party contract generation + e-signature flow, candidate pipeline UI, CUMULUS integration.

### Prototype Fidelity Decision

For the initial fork prototype, both Wise and CUMULUS integrations will be **fully mocked** (stubbed API responses) rather than built against real sandboxes. This unblocks development immediately instead of waiting on Wise Platform partnership approval or Shawn confirming whether CUMULUS has an API. Real integrations get swapped in once partnerships are in place.

### Repo Creation Approach

`lucidverify/` will be created one level up from `lucidledger/` via **copy-and-strip off a clean git ref** — not a raw working-tree copy (the Lucid Ledger working tree typically has unrelated in-flight changes that shouldn't travel over) and not a from-scratch build (would re-do scaffolding work that already exists and works). Only Verify-relevant notes travel over (this file, the Verité meeting note) — not the full business-strategy corpus.
